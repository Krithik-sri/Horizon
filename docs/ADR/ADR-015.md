# ADR-015: Spoken guidance ships, timed by distance to the maneuver rather than by detecting the corner

## Context

`ADR-012` excluded spoken guidance twice, for two different reasons, and both have to be answered
separately — this is the record's whole job.

The first was a deferral:

> "Spoken audio guidance is excluded for the reason `ADR-011` already gave: it is a
> `horizon-design`-scale question, not resolved by `routeProgress.ts` merely existing."
> (`ADR-012`, Decision §4)

The second is harder, because `ADR-012` did not merely defer it — it pre-emptively rejected the
argument that would most obviously be used to bring it back. When it turned down a haptic pulse, it
turned down the *reasoning*, not just the channel:

> "Genuinely tempting — haptic sits below Visual on the Attention Ladder … so it reads as the more
> conservative choice, not a louder one. **Rejected for the same reason as the sliding banner:** a
> haptic pulse is still a delivery event with a firing moment. Moving the event to a different
> channel doesn't remove the timing problem, it just makes the mistimed delivery a buzz instead of a
> slide." (`ADR-012`, Alternatives Considered)

Audio also sits below Visual on `docs/PRODUCT.md`'s ladder (`Ambient → Peripheral → Haptic → Audio →
Visual → Interrupt`). So **"audio is lower on the ladder" is not a valid argument here** — it is
verbatim the argument `ADR-012` already heard and rejected for haptics. Any record that reverses
`ADR-012` on that basis alone is just re-litigating a settled point with a different noun.

This record therefore rests on the *other* thing `ADR-012` said, in its rejection of the sliding
banner:

> "an event needs a rule for when it's allowed to fire — which means detecting whether the rider is
> mid-corner, which needs a sensor this app doesn't have."

That sentence contains a hidden assumption, and the assumption is wrong. Knowing whether the rider
is mid-corner does not require sensing the rider. **The corner's position is already in the data.**
`Step.wayPoints[0]` is the polyline index where the maneuver happens, and
`mobile/src/core/routeProgress.ts` already computes `metersToStep` — the rider's distance to exactly
that point — on every fix, and has since before `ADR-012` was written. The app cannot detect lean,
but it has never needed to: it can tell how far the rider is from the turn, which is the question
that actually matters for deciding whether it is safe to speak.

`ADR-012` also recorded a cost it chose to accept, and this record is the answer to it:

> "**A maneuver cue can be missed.** A rider who doesn't glance at the Ahead band at the right moment
> gets nothing louder to catch their attention." (`ADR-012`, Decision §2)

## Decision

### 1. Spoken maneuver cues ship, using `expo-speech`

On-device OS text-to-speech. No account, no API key, no network call, no cost — `CLAUDE.md` hard
constraint #1 holds without qualification, and unlike every other capability added recently this one
adds no load to the ORS quota `ADR-013` and `ADR-014` have been steadily filling.

It is a native module, so it requires a dev-client rebuild. That is the practical cost of this
record and it is the reason this phase was sequenced after the ones that hot-reload.

### 2. The firing rule is distance to the maneuver, not detection of the maneuver

Every announcement fires strictly *before* the turn, on the straight approach, and the rule that
guarantees it uses only `metersToStep` — a number the app already has. Three mechanisms, layered so
that no single mistake defeats all of them:

1. **Nothing is ever spoken while `progress.imminent` is true.** `imminent` means
   `metersToStep <= IMMINENT_METERS` (30 m) and is the app's existing, already-computed proxy for
   "the rider is at the corner." This guard lives inside the pure `dueTier` predicate rather than at
   the call site, so a future caller cannot forget it.
2. **The last announcement's threshold has a hard floor of 60 m** — twice `IMMINENT_METERS`. The
   final cue is therefore structurally finished speaking before the guard region begins, rather than
   merely usually finished.
3. **An in-flight utterance is cut** the moment the rider crosses into `imminent`, via
   `Speech.stop()`. Cutting produces silence, and silence cannot interrupt a corner.

   This is the only place the *guidance logic* cuts a cue — there is exactly one safety reason to
   stop speaking, and it is this one. That is a constraint on the firing rules, not on resource
   cleanup: `Speech.speak` is fire-and-forget at the native layer, so leaving the Motion screen or
   switching guidance off must also stop an utterance already under way, or a voice carries on
   announcing a turn over a screen that has no route. Ordinary teardown, and not a fourth mechanism.

This is what `ADR-012`'s sliding banner lacked. The banner had no principled firing rule available to
it, because a visual element arriving in peripheral vision is disruptive at *any* distance — there is
no distance at which a slide-in becomes safe. An utterance that completes 60 m before the turn is
finished before the rider is in it.

### 3. Thresholds and phrasing

| Tier | Fires at | Says |
|---|---|---|
| `far` | 800 m | "In 800 metres, turn right onto NH 44" |
| `near` | 200 m | "In 200 metres, turn right onto NH 44" |
| `final` | `clamp(speed × 5, 60, 250)` m | "Turn right onto NH 44" |

The final threshold is **speed-aware** rather than fixed. A constant 60 m is 2.4 seconds at 90 km/h,
which is too late to act on; five seconds of travel is the right unit, floored at 60 m so a crawling
rider still gets a cue and Decision §2's guarantee holds, capped at 250 m so a motorway cue does not
fire a quarter-kilometre early.

**Phrasing reuses `step.instruction` verbatim.** ORS already returns a readable, localized sentence;
composing our own from `type` + `name` would reinvent it worse, in more code, with a lookup table to
maintain. The distance tiers only prefix it.

**De-duplication** keys on `` `${step.wayPoints[0]}:${tier}` `` — `wayPoints[0]` is unique per step
within a route, and the set clears when the active route's array identity changes (the same trick
`routeProgress.ts`'s `cumCache` already uses to invalidate itself without a version field).

One subtlety that matters more than it looks: a tier fires only if the rider was ever *beyond* it for
this step. `routeProgress.check.ts` already asserts `metersToStep` decreases monotonically, so the
first value observed after a step becomes current is its maximum; storing that one number as
`entryMeters` and requiring `entryMeters > T` is what stops two maneuvers 150 m apart from producing
six announcements instead of two.

### 4. What is never spoken

- **Arrival.** Arriving is a Return-register moment, not something to say into a rider's helmet as
  they pull in. The visual card already says "Arrived".
- **Off-route.** A status, not a directive. Telling a rider who is already lost and looking for
  somewhere to turn around that they are lost is the interruption the ladder exists to prevent.
  `ADR-014` answers off-route by producing a route whose first cue is an ordinary maneuver cue, which
  is the useful form of the same information.

### 5. On by default, with the toggle in Departure only

Default-on is what parity means, and a rider who wants silence has an obvious expectation that the
app can be silenced. But the control lives in Departure. A mute toggle in Motion is a control
competing with the road, and reaching for it is exactly the behaviour `docs/PRODUCT.md`'s corner rule
is written to prevent. Departure's whole question is "am I ready to ride?" — audio preference is part
of that answer.

### 6. `ADR-012` §2's ambient visual cue is untouched

The card in the Ahead band keeps behaving exactly as `ADR-012` ratified: persistent, content-swapping,
never mounting or sliding. Speech is added alongside it, not in place of it. A rider with TTS
disabled, or on a device with no engine installed, sees precisely what they see today.

## Alternatives Considered

### Keep spoken guidance excluded; add a haptic pulse instead

`ADR-012` named this as the likely next lever if ambient-only cues proved insufficient, and it has a
real advantage this record should not pretend away: a haptic pulse works with the phone in a tank
bag, in a pocket, in a downpour, and through earplugs — none of which is true of audio. **Rejected**
because it does not solve the problem `ADR-012` identified with it. A pulse carries one bit: *look at
the screen.* So it does not replace the missed glance, it demands one, and it demands one at the
moment the rider is closest to the turn. Speech carries the instruction itself, so the rider never
needs to look at all — which is the actual goal. Worth revisiting as a *supplement* for riders who
cannot use audio; it is not a substitute.

### Compose the spoken phrase from `step.type` and `step.name`

More control over wording, and it would let the phrasing be tuned for speech ("N-H forty-four")
rather than for reading. **Rejected:** it means maintaining a second instruction generator alongside
ORS's, in every language ORS supports, to fix a pronunciation problem that is cosmetic. The honest
consequence is recorded below.

### Speak at fixed distances only, no speed-aware final tier

Simpler, one fewer tuned constant, and trivially predictable. **Rejected:** 60 m is 2.4 seconds at
motorway speed. The whole value of the final cue is that it arrives with time to act, and a fixed
distance cannot deliver that across the speed range a motorcycle actually covers.

### Duck or suppress speech near a corner instead of forbidding it

A softer version of Decision §2 — lower the volume rather than refuse to speak. **Rejected:** a
half-audible instruction mid-corner is worse than none, because it prompts the rider to attend to
something they cannot resolve. The binary rule is also the one that can be asserted in a check
script; "quieter" cannot.

### Wait for field evidence that ambient cues are actually being missed

`ADR-012` set that as the condition for revisiting, and the app is still unproven on a real device
(`CLAUDE.md`, Status), so strictly speaking the evidence does not exist yet. **Rejected** on the
owner's explicit instruction to build full navigation parity, and recorded here so the shortcut is
legible: this record ships a mitigation for a cost that was accepted in theory and never observed in
practice. If field use shows the ambient cue was sufficient all along, Decision §5's default is the
thing to change.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- `ADR-012`'s accepted cost — a maneuver cue can be missed — now has an answer that does not require
  the rider to look at anything.
- The corner rule holds by a rule that can be *checked*: `voiceGuidance.check.ts` asserts the
  `imminent` guard and the 60 m floor directly, which is stronger than `ADR-012` §2's guarantee,
  which holds by construction and therefore cannot be tested at all.
- No new service, no key, no quota. The first capability added in several records that costs ORS
  nothing.
- Riders with TTS off, or no engine installed, lose nothing relative to today.

**Negative**

- **This is the first delivery event in the Motion register.** `ADR-012` §2's guarantee was
  categorical — nothing arrives, ever. That is now qualified: nothing arrives *visually*, and audio
  arrives under a distance rule. The rule is defensible and layered, but the categorical version was
  simpler to reason about and it is genuinely gone.
- **`expo-speech` and LiveKit convoy voice will contend for one audio output.** Phase H connects a
  room and there is no coordination between the two; Android `expo-speech` has no audio-focus
  control. Not solved here. The likely answer is to suppress navigation speech while a LiveKit room
  has an active speaker, and that belongs in the voice record, not this one.
- **A dev-client rebuild is required.** This cannot ship as a JS reload, which makes it the first
  phase that cannot be verified end-to-end without a build.
- **ORS instruction text is written to be read, not spoken.** "NH 44" will be pronounced "N H
  forty-four", and route numbers, abbreviations and non-Latin place names will all be approximate.
  Accepted rather than fixed, per the rejected alternative above.
- **`imminent` measures distance to a polyline *vertex*, not to the turn apex** —
  `routeProgress.ts` already flags its vertex-snap approximation. A few metres of error against a
  60 m floor is comfortable margin, but the floor is doing real work and should not be lowered
  without revisiting that.

## Trade-offs

| We gave up | We got |
|---|---|
| `ADR-012` §2's categorical "no delivery events in Motion" | An instruction the rider can act on without looking at the screen |
| Control over spoken phrasing | No second instruction generator to maintain in every language ORS supports |
| One fewer tuned constant (a fixed final threshold) | A final cue that arrives with time to act at motorway speed as well as in town |
| A JS-only phase | On-device TTS with no account, no key and no quota |

## Future Revisions

Revisit if:

- **Phase H's convoy voice ships**, at which point the audio-contention question in Consequences must
  actually be answered rather than noted.
- **Riders report cues arriving too late.** The knob is `FINAL_LEAD_SECONDS`, not the 60 m floor —
  the floor is what makes Decision §2's guarantee hold and lowering it undoes this record's central
  argument.
- **Riders who cannot use audio** (earplugs, tank bag, deafness) need the cue. A haptic pulse as a
  *supplement* is the rejected alternative worth reopening, on different grounds than `ADR-012`
  rejected it.
- **Field use shows the ambient visual cue was sufficient**, in which case Decision §5's default-on
  is the thing to reconsider — not the feature's existence.

**What would *not* justify revisiting:** an argument that audio sits below Visual on the Attention
Ladder and is therefore safe. That argument is insufficient on its own and always was — `ADR-012`
rejected it for haptics, and Context above explains why this record does not lean on it either.
