## Context

Horizon is a hobby project for a friend group of cyclists. It has no revenue, no funding, and no
users to bill.

**The hard fact:** no credit card is available to the project. This is stated three times in the
existing documentation — `CLAUDE.md` ("**Hard constraint: no paid accounts and no credit card.**"),
`docs/SYSTEM_DESIGN.md` §2, and `docs/SYSTEM_DESIGN.md` §12 — which is unusual emphasis, and deliberate.

**The distinction that matters, and it is not the obvious one:** the constraint is not "spend $0". It
is "**no credit card can be put on file**". Those are very different filters:

- Google Maps Platform has a generous free monthly credit, and you cannot use a single tile of it
  without billing enabled and a valid card.
- Mapbox, Twilio, Agora, and most AWS/GCP services are the same shape: $0 is achievable, but only
  after a card is registered.
- OpenFreeMap needs nothing at all. OpenRouteService and LiveKit Cloud need an email address.

So the filter is **not** on price. It is on *whether a payment instrument is required to begin*. That
filter eliminates most of the commercial cloud, including several services that would genuinely have
been free for our usage.

There is a second reason this constraint is valuable, beyond necessity: **a card on file is an
open-ended liability.** A misconfigured loop, a leaked key, or a viral moment turns into a bill
nobody can pay. With no card anywhere, the worst case is a service stops working. For a hobby
project maintained in spare time, "it stopped working" is a strictly better failure mode than "it
generated an invoice."

## Decision

**Every third-party service in Horizon must be free with at most an email signup. No credit card,
anywhere, for any reason.**

This is a **hard constraint**, not a preference. A PR that introduces a card-required service is
rejected regardless of technical merit — and the technical merit is often real, which is exactly why
this needs to be written down rather than argued case by case.

### The full service inventory, and what each costs

| Service | Purpose | Signup | Card | ADR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OpenFreeMap** | Map tiles | **none** | ❌ | [003](./ADR-003.md) |
| **OpenRouteService** | Cycling directions | email | ❌ | [003](./ADR-003.md) |
| **LiveKit Cloud** | Group voice SFU | email | ❌ | [005](./ADR-005.md) |
| **Koyeb / Cloudflare Tunnel** | Backend hosting + HTTPS | email | ❌ | HZ-7 |
| **GitHub** | Source, and CI when it lands | email | ❌ | — |

**Total recurring cost: $0.** Every one of these has an open-source self-hostable equivalent, so none
is a permanent lock-in.

### Standing rules that follow

1. **Named prohibitions** (`CLAUDE.md`): no Mapbox, no Google Maps, no paid APIs. These are called
   out by name because they are the defaults a contributor would reach for.
2. **Every dependency needs a self-hosting escape hatch.** MapLibre, OpenFreeMap tiles, ORS, and
   LiveKit are all open source. This is *why* those specific vendors won — not just that they're free
   today, but that we can leave if they stop being free.
3. **The only money in the project is optional app-store distribution** — Apple $99/yr, Google $25
   once (`docs/SYSTEM_DESIGN.md` §12) — and [ADR-004](./ADR-004.md)'s PWA-first decision avoids even that
   for v1.
4. **Free tiers are treated as a courtesy, not an entitlement.** ORS's daily cap is respected by
   fetching a route **once per ride, not once per rider** (`CLAUDE.md`). OpenFreeMap is
   donation-funded, so runtime tile caching (HZ-29) is on the board partly out of politeness.
5. **No database, no Redis, no managed auth in v1** — these are `docs/SYSTEM_DESIGN.md` §9's scaling path.
   Every one of them is a hosting bill.

## Alternatives Considered

### Use free tiers that require a card, but stay under the limit

The pragmatic position, and the one most teams take: register a card, set billing alerts, use Google
Maps' free credit and Twilio's trial, and simply don't exceed them.

**Rejected on both prongs.** First, factually: **no card is available**, so this isn't a choice we
get to make. Second, even if one were, it reintroduces the open-ended liability that makes this
constraint valuable — a leaked ORS-equivalent key or a runaway retry loop becomes a bill. Billing
alerts fire *after* the spend.

### Accept a small monthly cost (a $5 VPS)

`docs/SYSTEM_DESIGN.md` §12 explicitly notes a $5/mo VPS would run the single Go binary comfortably, and
it would also unlock self-hosted tiles and a self-hosted SFU. Genuinely the least-bad paid option.

**Rejected:** it requires a card, and it converts a zero-commitment hobby project into a recurring
obligation. A project that costs nothing survives indefinite neglect. A project with a monthly bill
gets cancelled the first month nobody rides.

### Self-host everything on hardware we already own

A home server or a spare machine — no recurring cost, complete control, no rate limits, no third
parties at all. Combined with Cloudflare Tunnel, it wouldn't even need a static IP.

**Rejected for v1**, though it's the most philosophically aligned option. It trades money for
operational burden: uptime becomes our problem, a home connection's upload bandwidth is poor for
media relay, and running tiles + ORS + an SFU is far more work than the 340-line binary that is
currently the entire backend. **This is the documented escape hatch** if free tiers disappear —
`docs/SYSTEM_DESIGN.md` §9 step 4.

### Build a "cost-aware" abstraction layer over each service

Provider-agnostic interfaces so any vendor can be swapped when a free tier ends.

**Rejected as speculative generality**
([P6](../DEVELOPMENT_GUIDE.md#p6--simplicity-over-cleverness)) — an abstraction with one
implementation. We achieved the same outcome more cheaply by choosing vendors whose swap cost is
already low: the tile provider is *one style URL string*, and ORS is isolated behind a backend
package because the key must stay server-side anyway. Design for replaceability by keeping the
surface small, not by adding a layer.

### Drop features that can't be built for free

Cut voice; ship location and standings only.

**Rejected because it turned out to be unnecessary** — LiveKit's free tier covers group voice for 15
people with no card. But it was a genuine option, and it remains the fallback if LiveKit's terms
change: **cut the feature rather than start paying.** That ordering is the constraint doing its job.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- **The project costs nothing to build, run, or maintain.** It can be neglected for a year and still
  work.
- **Onboarding is nearly frictionless.** OpenFreeMap needs no key at all, so a contributor clones the
  repo and gets a working map — no signup, no `.env` entry. Only Phases 2 and 3 need credentials, and
  both are email-only.
- **It forced better architecture.** Nearly every constraint-driven choice here is *independently*
  good:
  - MapLibre over Mapbox → a BSD-licensed renderer that cannot be relicensed out from under us.
  - Unbundled tiles/renderer/directions → three independently replaceable pieces instead of one
    vendor SDK.
  - No database → in-memory rooms, which are genuinely correct at 15 riders and much simpler.
  - PWA first → faster iteration and distribution by URL.
  - Self-hostable everything → no permanent lock-in anywhere in the stack.
- **No liability.** No card means no surprise bill, ever. The worst case is degraded functionality.
- **Decisions get made faster.** "Does it need a card?" eliminates most of the option space in one
  question, and eliminating options is most of what makes small teams fast.
- **It scopes the project honestly.** This is a friend-group hobby app for ≤15 riders. The constraint
  keeps the architecture matched to that reality instead of drifting toward a product nobody asked
  for.

**Negative**

- **We depend on donation-funded community infrastructure.** OpenFreeMap and ORS have no SLA, no
  support, and no uptime guarantee. If OpenFreeMap goes down mid-ride, the basemap goes blank.
- **Free tiers can change or vanish at any time**, unilaterally. LiveKit Cloud's terms are a
  commercial decision we have no input into.
- **Hard limits exist and are real.** ORS enforces a daily cap per key. Nothing in the code currently
  *enforces* the once-per-ride rule — it's a convention that HZ-10 must implement.
- **Some capabilities are simply unavailable.** Turn-by-turn navigation, geocoding and search,
  guaranteed-uptime hosting, and managed auth are all card-required or self-host-only. That's why
  turn-by-turn is an explicit non-goal.
- **Data quality is what OSM has**, which is excellent in some regions and patchy in others. Google
  would be better in places.
- **Free hosting tiers cold-start and have low memory ceilings.** Combined with the room leak
  (BUG-03), that's a real operational risk once HZ-7 deploys — and it is part of why HZ-3 is Critical.
- **The self-hosting escape hatch costs money to exercise.** The hatch exists, but pulling it means
  breaking this ADR. It is an option, not a free option.

## Trade-offs

| We gave up | We got |
|---|---|
| Google's map data quality and turn-by-turn | $0 forever, and no liability of any kind |
| Commercial SLAs and support | An architecture with no permanent vendor lock-in |
| Managed databases, auth, and hosting | Onboarding with no signup for the core experience |
| Several technically superior options ([001](./ADR-001.md), [003](./ADR-003.md), [005](./ADR-005.md)) | Decisions that were fast to make and cheap to reverse |
| Guaranteed uptime | A project that survives indefinite neglect |

**The honest summary:** this constraint began as a limitation and became a design principle. Most of
what it forced — open-source components, unbundled services, self-hostable dependencies, a small
in-memory backend — is what we would want anyway at this scale. The genuine losses are map data
quality in some regions and the absence of any uptime guarantee.

## Future Revisions

This constraint holds **for as long as Horizon is a friend-group hobby app.** That is the whole
condition.

Revisit when the project changes category:

- **It acquires users who are not friends.** Real users imply real expectations — uptime, support,
  privacy guarantees, data retention. That requires paid infrastructure and an entity to own it, and
  it means revisiting `docs/SYSTEM_DESIGN.md` §9's scaling path in full: auth, then persistence, then
  horizontal scale.
- **It acquires revenue.** Then cost stops being a constraint and becomes a budget.
- **A free tier disappears or degrades below usability.** Handle these **in this order**, and note
  that the first three options all preserve the constraint:
  1. **Switch to another free provider.** Tiles → MapTiler or Stadia (a one-line style URL change).
  2. **Reduce usage** — cache more aggressively, fetch less often, degrade gracefully.
  3. **Cut the feature.** Voice is the obvious candidate: it's the only one fully independent of the
     location pipe.
  4. **Only then** self-host, or pay. This step breaks the ADR and requires a superseding record.
- **App-store distribution becomes necessary.** Apple's $99/yr is the smallest possible breach and
  would be its own decision. [ADR-004](./ADR-004.md)'s PWA-first choice defers it indefinitely.

**How to supersede this ADR:** write a new one that states what changed about the project (not about
the technology), what specifically will be paid for, what the monthly ceiling is, and who owns the
account. **Do not amend this record** — the constraint's history is the point, because four other
ADRs cite it as their deciding factor.

**What would *not* justify revisiting:** wanting better map data, wanting an SLA, wanting a managed
database, or a contributor offering to pay for something personally. The last one is the most
tempting and the most corrosive — it makes the project depend on one person's continued goodwill,
which is a worse dependency than any donation-funded tile server.
