# ADR-020: Convoy voice is exempt from the corner rule — the app never mutes, ducks, or delays a co-rider

| | |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Accepted |
| **Date** | 2026-08-10 |
| **Deciders** | Horizon team |
| **Supersedes** | — |
| **Amends** | [ADR-015](./ADR-015.md) (Consequences → the unanswered audio-contention item: this record answers it) — ADR-005's choice of LiveKit and its token design are implemented here unchanged, not amended |

## Context

`ADR-005` chose LiveKit and specified the token shape; `backend/main.go` has answered
`POST /rides/{code}/voice-token` with a hardcoded 501 ever since. All four LiveKit packages are
installed, `mobile/app.config.ts` registers the plugin and declares the microphone permission, and
no code uses any of it. This record is about the part `ADR-005` did not decide: **what voice is
allowed to do to a rider's attention.**

That question is now urgent for a reason that did not exist when `ADR-005` was written.
`CLAUDE.md` hard constraint #3 says *"No non-safety information reaches the rider while they are
turning, leaning, braking, or otherwise busy."* `ADR-012` satisfied it for maneuver cues by removing
the delivery event; `ADR-015` satisfied it for speech by timing every utterance against
`metersToStep`. Both records solved it by controlling *when the app speaks*.

Neither approach applies to a human being. The app does not choose when a co-rider starts talking,
and it cannot know whether what they are saying is safety-critical — "slowing, gravel ahead" and
"nice weather" arrive through the same pipe.

`ADR-015` also left an explicit debt:

> "`expo-speech` and LiveKit convoy voice will contend for one audio output. … The likely answer is
> to suppress navigation speech while a LiveKit room has an active speaker, and that belongs in the
> voice record, not this one." (`ADR-015`, Consequences)

Decision §5 pays that debt.

## Decision

### 1. Voice is exempt from the corner rule

The app does **not** mute, duck, delay, or buffer a co-rider's transmission, at any point, for any
reason — including while `progress.imminent` is true.

Three reasons, and the third is decisive:

1. **It is not app-generated information.** The corner rule governs what *Horizon* puts in front of
   a rider. A conversation between riders is not Horizon's output any more than a passenger talking
   is the helmet's output.
2. **It is Audio, and continuous rather than eventful.** There is no delivery event to mistime —
   structurally the same property `ADR-012` §2 used to make the maneuver cue corner-safe.
3. **The app cannot tell safety-critical speech from chatter, and guessing wrong is asymmetric.**
   Suppressing "slowing, gravel ahead" to protect a rider from a distraction is a strictly worse
   outcome than the distraction. The corner rule exists to keep a rider safe; applying it here would
   invert it.

**Explicitly rejected, because it will be proposed:** gating the microphone on `progress.imminent`.
It sounds like consistency with `ADR-015`. It would cut a rider's own transmission in the 30 m
before a turn — which is precisely the moment the most safety-relevant sentence a rider ever sends
gets spoken. The app would be clipping "slowing, left ahead" for the rider's own good.

What the corner rule *does* still forbid here is any new **visual** element arriving during a
maneuver. That is why incoming voice has no UI at all: no speaking-rider list, no level meter, no
participant count. Nothing about someone talking changes a single pixel.

### 2. The push-to-talk surface is the invisible bottom band

`register.motion.touchTarget` is 56 pt, but 56 pt is a floor for a fingertip, not a design for a
gloved hand at 80 km/h with eyes on the road. **The only control findable by feel is a screen edge.**

`HorizonLine` in Motion lays out Ahead and Now across `0 → horizon.now` (0.58), so the bottom 42% of
the screen is bare map. The PTT control is a transparent, full-width `Pressable` occupying that band
that **renders nothing at rest**. On press-in the microphone opens and the only visual is a 2 pt
`amber.core` hairline along the very bottom edge — peripheral, at the edge of vision, gone on
release.

Zero pixels at rest satisfies "almost invisible." "The bottom of the screen" is operable without
looking. And the hairline is *rider-initiated*, so it can never surprise anyone — it appears only in
response to a press the rider is already making, which is why it does not count as a visual arriving
mid-corner.

### 3. Connect on Motion mount, microphone muted, always subscribed

Straight from `ADR-005`: connect with audio off so the mic starts muted. Subscription is always on,
so a rider hears their convoy from the first metre without any gesture — that is the actual value of
the feature.

Lazy-connect-on-first-press was considered: it saves battery for silent riders and would double as
the user gesture iOS requires for audio. Rejected because it eats the first sentence every time, and
the first sentence is usually the one that mattered. Android-first (`ADR-007`); revisit at the iOS
port.

### 4. Token minting, TTL, and refresh

Minted with `github.com/livekit/protocol/auth` — not hand-rolled. `CLAUDE.md`'s "do not hand-roll
JWT" rule is about *verification* and does not strictly apply to minting, where we choose the
algorithm, hold the secret, and no attacker-controlled bytes reach the signature. But `ADR-005`
already decided this on the record, and overruling an accepted ADR to save roughly 25 lines is a bad
trade.

**TTL is one hour, and the client re-mints on `RoomEvent.Disconnected`.** `ADR-005` asked for a short
TTL; taken literally (ten minutes) voice dies in the first tunnel and never returns, because LiveKit
validates `exp` at join *and* at reconnect. One hour plus a re-fetch on disconnect keeps the
short-TTL intent and survives mobile networks — the same reality that gave `wsClient.ts` its jittered
backoff.

Room name is the ride code; identity is the rider's `sub` (`ADR-017`). Grants are `RoomJoin`,
publish, subscribe — and **`CanPublishData: false`**, because position has its own pipe and a second
one would be an invitation to bypass the protocol in `CLAUDE.md`.

### 5. Navigation speech yields to human speech — by skipping, never by delaying

The answer to `ADR-015`'s open item.

When a remote participant is speaking and a navigation cue becomes due, the cue is **skipped for
that fix and retried on the next one**. It is not queued, not delayed, and not overlapped.

This reuses machinery that already exists rather than adding any: `useVoiceGuidance` already defers a
due cue while its own utterance is in flight, and already declines to mark a deferred tier as spoken
so the next fix retries it. Extending that same condition from "we are speaking" to "we or a co-rider
are speaking" is a one-line change to an existing predicate.

**Skipping rather than delaying is the safety-relevant part.** A delayed cue would fire whenever the
human stopped talking, which could be inside the corner — defeating `ADR-015` §2's entire guarantee.
A skipped cue simply retries, and if the rider has crossed into `imminent` by then, `dueTier`'s
guard returns null and nothing is spoken at all. The visual cue in the Ahead band is unaffected
throughout, so the instruction is still on screen — which is exactly the missable-cue cost `ADR-012`
§2 already accepted.

Priority between the two is therefore: **the human always wins.**

### 6. When voice is unavailable, render nothing

If the backend answers 503 (LiveKit env unset) or the token fetch fails, no PTT control is rendered
at all. An affordance that does nothing is worse than no affordance — `docs/PRODUCT.md`'s Confidence
pillar, *"if information cannot be trusted, it should not be shown,"* applied to controls.

## Alternatives Considered

### Mute or duck the microphone near a maneuver

Consistent with how every other channel in this app is treated, and defensible on a plain reading of
hard constraint #3. **Rejected** per Decision §1 — it clips the transmission most likely to be
safety-critical, at the exact moment it matters.

### Duck incoming voice volume during a maneuver rather than muting

Softer, and it keeps some signal. **Rejected** for the reason `ADR-015` rejected ducked speech: a
half-audible sentence is worse than either alternative, because it prompts the rider to attend to
something they cannot resolve.

### Buffer incoming voice and replay it after the corner

Nothing is lost, and nothing arrives at a bad moment. **Rejected** — it converts a conversation into
asynchronous voice messages. Two riders would be talking past each other with a variable delay, which
is worse than either real-time or silence, and it is the app editing a human exchange.

### Delay navigation speech until the room goes quiet, instead of skipping it

The cue is not lost. **Rejected** in Decision §5: a delayed cue fires when the human stops, which
may be inside the corner. This is the option that looks kinder and breaks the safety guarantee.

### A visible, labelled PTT button

Discoverable, and obvious to a first-time user. **Rejected** against the Motion register — a labelled
control competes with the road, and a rider who has to *look* for the talk button is looking at the
wrong thing. The bottom edge is discoverable once, in Departure, and by feel forever after.

### Show who is currently speaking

Genuinely useful in a group, and every conferencing product does it. **Rejected** as the one thing
Decision §1 does still forbid: a visual element that arrives at a moment the app does not control,
which is the corner-rule violation in its purest form.

## Consequences

**Positive**

- The most safety-relevant channel in the app is the one the app never interferes with.
- `ADR-015`'s open audio-contention item is answered, using a predicate that already exists rather
  than new machinery.
- Zero pixels at rest, and no visual ever arrives from voice — so the Motion register's guarantee is
  unchanged in the only sense the corner rule cares about.
- `notImplemented` is deleted from `main.go`; voice-token was its only caller.

**Negative**

- **A rider can be talked over by their convoy during a maneuver**, and the app will not help. That
  is the decision, and it is a real cost — this record asserts the alternative is worse, not that the
  cost is zero.
- **A navigation cue can be lost entirely** to a talkative convoy: skipped, retried, then suppressed
  by the `imminent` guard. The visual cue remains, which is the mitigation, and it is the same
  missable-cue trade `ADR-012` §2 already took.
- **The PTT band swallows map long-press in the bottom 42%.** `ride/[code].tsx`'s `handleLongPress` →
  `setDestination` becomes unreachable there. Long-press elsewhere still works, and the planner
  (`ADR-013`) is now the primary way to set a destination — but it is a real regression and belongs
  in the PR description, not in a bug report later.
- **Battery.** Continuously subscribed WebRTC on top of continuous high-accuracy GPS and a keep-awake
  lock is the heaviest state this app will ever run. `ADR-005` already recorded it as unmeasured, and
  it still is.
- **The token is only as strong as the join code** until a ride is bound to authenticated
  membership. `ADR-005` flagged this; `ADR-017` narrows it (the endpoint now requires a valid
  Supabase JWT, so it is no longer anonymous), but any authenticated user who learns a six-character
  code can still join that room's voice.
- `github.com/livekit/protocol` is a large module and takes the backend from two dependencies to
  three. `ADR-005` accepted this; `ADR-017` used the second. Worth noting the budget is spent.
- **`pion/webrtc` lands in `go.sum` even though nothing here does WebRTC**, which is genuinely
  surprising and would otherwise be discovered by a puzzled `go mod why` months from now. Choosing
  `protocol/auth` over `server-sdk-go` does *not* avoid it: `go mod why github.com/pion/webrtc/v4`
  reports `backend → protocol/auth → protocol/utils → pion/webrtc/v4`. The token package pulls it
  transitively and there is no lighter path to LiveKit-compatible minting short of hand-rolling the
  JWT, which `ADR-005` ruled out. Recorded, not fixed.

## Trade-offs

| We gave up | We got |
|---|---|
| A uniform application of the corner rule across every channel | A safety channel the app never clips, and no guessing about which sentence mattered |
| Any UI for incoming voice — speaker list, level meter, count | No visual can arrive at a moment the app does not control |
| A discoverable, labelled talk button | A control operable by feel with a glove, at speed, without looking |
| Navigation cues that always get spoken | The human always wins, without a delay that could fire inside a corner |

## Future Revisions

Revisit if:

- **The iOS port lands**, which forces the lazy-connect question again (iOS requires a user gesture
  before audio) and may change Decision §3.
- **Battery measurement shows continuous subscription is untenable**, in which case
  connect-on-first-press is the fallback and the cost is the first sentence.
- **Riders report navigation cues being lost to conversation** often enough to matter. The lever is
  the visual cue's prominence, not the audio priority — Decision §5's ordering is not the thing to
  change.
- **A ride gains authenticated membership** rather than a shared code, which closes the room-access
  gap `ADR-005` flagged.

**What would *not* justify revisiting:** an argument that voice should be muted near a corner for
consistency with `ADR-012` and `ADR-015`. Consistency is the argument this record exists to reject —
those decisions govern what the app says, and this one governs what a person says.
